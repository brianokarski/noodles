# Typography Principles

> Sources: Emil Ruder *Typography*, Müller-Brockmann *Grid Systems*, Muggeridge *Typography With/Without Words*, Typeface Design 101, Swiss layout references

---

## Core Rules

### 1. Constraint over abundance
Restrict typefaces to 1-2 families and 4-6 sizes max. The combinatorial possibilities within a limited palette are already infinite. Adding more creates noise, not range.

### 2. Make distinctions obvious or don't make them
Move from regular to bold, not regular to medium. If two elements are different, make the difference unmistakable. Subtle, ambiguous variation is the enemy of clarity.

### 3. Typography's sole duty is to convey information
A printed work that cannot be read becomes a product without purpose. Legibility is the non-negotiable baseline. Serve the content, not the ego.

### 4. Form and counter-form are inseparable
The relationship between printed area and white space must be clear and unequivocal. The unprinted space is as designed as the printed. Design the negative space, not just the elements.

### 5. Depersonalization is a virtue
The typographer must take an impersonal view — willful individuality and emotion have limited place. Let the system speak.

## Type Scale

- Use **extreme scale contrast** — 10:1 or greater ratio between headlines and body text
- Headlines: 96-180px (display), tight tracking (-3 to -6px)
- Section labels: 11-14px, wide letter-spacing (4-8px), uppercase
- Body: 16-18px, comfortable line-height (1.5-1.7x)
- Captions/metadata: 11-12px, medium weight, spaced tracking

## Tracking & Leading

- **The longer the line length, the more leading required.** Type size, line length, and leading form an interdependent triad — change one, and the others must respond.
- Body text: 45-75 characters per line for optimal readability
- Display type: tighten tracking as size increases (-1px per ~20px of font size)
- Small caps / labels: open tracking (4-8px letter-spacing) for legibility and authority
- Leading should create consistent "grey value" — squint at layouts to assess tonal weight of text blocks

## Hierarchy Patterns

- **Weight, size, spacing, and position are the only tools for hierarchy.** No color-coding, no icons, no bullets — pure typographic structure.
- Establish reading order through scale contrast, not decoration
- Use systematic repetition: once a layout system is established, apply it consistently
- Information hierarchy: section number (ghost/background) → section label (small, accented) → headline (massive) → body → metadata

## Typeface Selection

- **Sans-serif grotesques** for the Swiss canon: Helvetica, Akzidenz-Grotesk, Inter (screen equivalent)
- **x-height drives legibility at small sizes** — prioritize fonts with large x-height relative to body size
- What is mathematically equal is not visually equal — optical compensation is mandatory (round forms need overshoot, padding around round elements should be slightly less than square)

## Do's and Don'ts

**Do:**
- Flush-left alignment (ragged right) — natural word spacing over forced justification
- Print/preview at actual size on target device
- Design in context (double-spread, user flow) — never a single screen in isolation
- Craft line endings carefully — avoid widows and orphans
- Test grey value by squinting at layouts

**Don't:**
- Use many different typefaces in multitudes of slightly different sizes
- Create styles that are only subtly different from each other
- Evaluate type in isolation — always place it in realistic layouts with real content
- Confuse technical novelty with good design — shoddy craftsmanship disguised in contemporary style causes confusion
- Fill every space — emptiness is emphasis
