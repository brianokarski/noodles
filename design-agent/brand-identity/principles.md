# Brand Identity Principles

> Sources: Alina Wheeler *Designing Brand Identity*, K-HOLE #1 *Fragmoretation*, Debbie Millman, McLuhan

---

## Core Rules

### 1. Saying who you aren't is as important as saying who you are
Limits localize you. Uniqueness relies on what you DON'T do. Constraint signals intentionality and taste.

### 2. Stay on message — one distinctive voice everywhere
Project the same unified message across all channels. A unified message is memorable, identifiable, and customer-centered.

### 3. Clarity, brevity, precision
Cut through the clutter to produce soundbites. Edit out modifying phrases, adverbs, and extraneous text.

### 4. Design for incompleteness
"Where our product ends, you begin." The best brand experiences leave space for the consumer to complete the meaning.

### 5. Balance consistency and flexibility
Design applications to allow for flexibility while maintaining a consistent visual language — recognition across diverse applications without becoming stale.

## Strategic Positioning

- **Negative positioning wins** — define by what you withhold, not what you display. Obscured, hidden, requiring effort to decode.
- **Fragmentation as attention strategy** — catch the eye by being broken-off, hidden, decontextualized, not by being big or flashy
- **Distribution is not branding** — accessibility and ubiquity succeed through distribution, not the enigmatic force of branding
- **Product as experience, not lifestyle** — market products as fragments of a lifestyle defined by the consumer

## Visual Identity

- **Every decision must have a reason** — remove any nonessential element
- Animation/motion should evolve from the existing visual language, not be imposed
- Use real text, never Lorem Ipsum — the design must work with actual content
- Show designs at actual size to assess legibility and impact
- Consider every surface — the back of a card, the bottom of a page, the footer of a site
- Develop system formats (templates) for all touchpoints

## Color Strategy

- Develop a deliberate color strategy aligned with brand values
- A limited palette signals intentionality — constraint = quality
- Core palette + adaptable secondary colors for flexibility
- Color impacts recognition and creates emotional connection

## K-HOLE Framework: Fragmoretation vs Flatmentation

| Fragmoretation (aspire to) | Flatmentation (avoid) |
|---|---|
| Secrecy | Disclosure |
| Somewhere | Everywhere |
| Discretion | Ostentation |
| Opacity | Transparency |
| Longing | Satisfaction |
| Delay | Immediacy |
| Segmentation | Continuity |

## Do's and Don'ts

**Do:**
- Design for restraint — not every element visible at once
- Use progressive disclosure and information layering
- Fragment to focus — each screen/page does one thing exceptionally
- Examine multiple iterations before finalizing
- White space as strategic opacity — emptiness communicates confidence

**Don't:**
- Over-disclose — ubiquity dilutes potency
- Design complete "lifestyle" narratives — leave space for the audience
- Use placeholder content when real content is available
- Ignore the back/reverse/secondary surfaces
- Confuse distribution reach with brand strength
